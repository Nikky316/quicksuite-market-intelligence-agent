# Reliability evaluation and lessons learned

## Confidence framework

| Insight | Source quality | Consistency | Timeliness | Overall confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selected listed prices | Brand pages support advertised observations, not realized prices | Several reported ₹199 observations; marketplace comparison missing | Reported access September 2026; recheck before a decision | Medium for the bounded observation |
| Unresolved requirements | Official search source plus older secondary reporting; no applicable instrument established | Search failure does not corroborate absence | Older reporting cannot establish current status | Medium for the documented research gap |
| Complaint examples | Traceable product IDs/reviews, but self-selected and uncontrolled | Qualitative overlap across different products/platforms; mixed and positive reviews also exist | Internal collection date and relevance to current models unconfirmed | Low for commercial significance |
| Warranty hypothesis | Reported model-specific terms from product/policy pages | Two brands do not establish a category-wide gap | Reported September 2026 access; exact model/seller terms need rechecking | Medium for selected terms |

Confidence in an observed page or quoted complaint differs from confidence in its business implication. Overall readiness for inventory commitment remains Low.

## Corrections that mattered

| Earlier problem | Correction | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| URL timestamps treated as the collection date | Collection date remains unconfirmed | Avoids presenting stale or undated data as current |
| Distinct product count exceeded a category's row count | Rebuilt within-category counts and reconciled totals | Exposes a mathematical inconsistency instead of rationalizing it |
| Duplicate records described as equivalent | Flagged conflicting prices and rating counts; documented deduplication | Prevents accidental double counting and hidden selection choices |
| Commas treated as reliable review boundaries | Review text retained as embedded samples | Avoids invented individual-review counts and prevalence |
| Brand-site price described as a market price floor | Restricted to the lowest observed selected listed price | Preserves product and channel boundaries |
| Failure to locate a rule treated as proof of absence | Recorded legal status as unresolved | Keeps an incomplete search from becoming a legal conclusion |
| Reviews treated as evidence of a market-wide quality gap | Used as test hypotheses | Separates anecdote from representative evidence |

## What would change the decision

- Verified comparable offers and full cost estimates would test pricing viability.
- Structured customer research would test demand and willingness to pay.
- Candidate product tests would establish whether performance claims can be supported.
- Model-specific warranty terms and service-cost estimates would test the warranty proposition.
- Authoritative product-specific guidance would resolve applicable requirements.

## Learning outcome

The workflow's value was the combination of research and scrutiny: tracing claims, rejecting unsupported explanations, qualifying conclusions and defining what leadership should verify next. A project pass does not independently validate the commercial opportunity.
